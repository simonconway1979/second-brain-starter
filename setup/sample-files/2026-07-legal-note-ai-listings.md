# Legal note: AI-generated content in seller listings

**Author:** Catarina Moura, Senior Legal Counsel
**Requested by:** Tomás Reis, Head of Product
**Date:** 14 July 2026
**Status:** Internal advice · Privileged and confidential
**Tags:** legal, AI, listings, sellers

---

## Question

Product asked whether Rehanga can use AI to write or fill in parts of a seller's listing (title, description, condition, brand) and what conditions apply.

## Short answer

Yes, with three conditions. The listing has to stay the seller's statement about their own item, and buyers shouldn't be misled about who said what.

## Advice

**1. The seller reviews and confirms before publishing.**
AI-drafted text must be reviewed and confirmed by the seller before the listing goes live. An active step is needed: the seller sees the draft and confirms it. Publishing AI text automatically, or with a pre-ticked confirmation, doesn't meet this.

**2. Label AI assistance.**
Where AI helped write the listing, we should say so. A short label on the listing (for example "Description drafted with AI, confirmed by the seller") is enough. The label should be visible to buyers, not only in the seller's settings.

**3. Don't present AI text as the seller's own statement without confirmation.**
Condition and brand matter most here. If a buyer reads "Very good condition" or a brand name, they will take it as the seller's claim. Unless the seller has confirmed it, we can't present it that way. If the model isn't sure of something, the listing should not state it as fact.

## Why

- Our Trust & Safety policy makes the seller responsible for the accuracy of a listing. That position is only fair, and only holds up, if the seller actually made the statements in it.
- If Rehanga writes the content and the seller doesn't check it, Rehanga starts to look like the author. That weakens our position in claims and complaints and could shift responsibility toward us.
- Consumer protection rules across our four markets are strict about misleading product information. Our advice is to treat AI-drafted listing text the same way we'd treat any content we produce ourselves.

## What this means for design

- A confirmation step for condition is the minimum. Confirming the whole draft in one tap is acceptable if the seller can see and edit every field first.
- Keep a record of what the AI drafted and what the seller confirmed or changed. We may need it in a claim.
- Don't use words like "authentic" or "genuine" in AI-drafted text.

## Not covered

- Pricing suggestions. Happy to look at these separately; the risk is lower.
- Use of seller photos to train models. Separate note to follow with the DPO.

Contact Catarina for anything unclear. Please link this note in any PRD that uses AI-generated listing content.
